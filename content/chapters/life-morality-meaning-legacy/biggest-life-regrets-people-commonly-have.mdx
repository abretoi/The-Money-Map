Regret research tends to circle the same handful of themes, and this map lays
them out as a scorecard rather than a list of platitudes. Each regret gets
paired with what it actually looks like day to day and what people wish they
had chosen instead — turning something abstract into something checkable.

The four things people realize too late — that time is the real wealth, that
presence beats perfection, that courage compounds, and that nobody wishes they
had worried more — read less like advice and more like a summary of hindsight
across many lives.

The No-Regrets Playbook and Daily Reminders sections translate that hindsight
into things doable *today*, not someday: a phone call, a dish you've been
saving, a trip you keep postponing. The map's closing line makes the trade
explicit — the regret is rarely having done too much, it's having waited.
