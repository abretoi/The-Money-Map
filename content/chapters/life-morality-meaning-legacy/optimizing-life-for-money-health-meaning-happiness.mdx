Most financial advice optimizes for one variable: money. This map treats
money as one of five pillars, alongside body, mind, relationships, and
spirit/meaning, and argues that neglecting the other four eventually
undermines the first.

The Balanced Life Scorecard turns that argument into something checkable —
eight concrete habits spanning sleep, movement, nutrition, savings
discipline, purposeful work, relationships, reflection, and rest. None of
these are exotic; the map's claim is that consistency across all eight
compounds, while intensity in only one or two does not.

The Imbalance Warning Signs section is the diagnostic half of the map. High
income with low peace, burnout, lifestyle inflation, constant comparison,
and isolation are named as specific, recognizable failure modes — not vague
warnings but patterns to check against your own life directly.

This map has no numeric financial figures beyond the scorecard's time-based
targets, so it carries no advisor disclaimer; its currency is habits, not
dollars.
