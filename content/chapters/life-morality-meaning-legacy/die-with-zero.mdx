Most financial advice optimizes for the biggest possible ending balance. This
map argues that goal is often wrong — that the point of money is to convert it
into a life well-lived while you still can.

The Big Idea section sets up the core tension: life energy is finite, so
spending and saving should be matched to the stage of life you're actually
in, not deferred indefinitely. The Key Learnings extend that into eight ideas
about timing, memory dividends, and estate sizing, and the Practical
Playbook sequences a path from securing the basics through to intentional
giving and legacy planning.

The Common Money Traps section names the failure mode directly: saving
without end, waiting too long to live, ignoring the value of experiences, and
leaving timing out of the plan altogether. Read together with the closing
takeaways, the map's argument is less "spend more" and more "spend on
purpose, on a clock that is actually running."
