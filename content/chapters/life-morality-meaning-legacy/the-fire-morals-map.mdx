The FIRE Morals Map is the philosophical companion to the retirement maps. Where
those focus on mechanics, this one distills twenty rules — and nearly all of
them point back to a single idea: **flexibility beats precision.**

The foundation morals are about the math (expenses set the target, savings rate
sets the speed, fixed costs matter most). The tax-and-access morals are about
reaching your money early without penalty. But the resilience column is where
the map earns its name: a plan that only *barely* works is fragile, so you build
margin, hold a buffer, keep spending variable, and draw from multiple buckets.

The life-design column is the reminder that the number is not the point.
Retiring *from* work is easy; retiring *into* a meaningful life is the harder,
more important design problem — which is why the closing line frames FIRE as
building enough margin and meaning to live on your own terms.
