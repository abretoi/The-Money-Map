keyTakeaways:
  - A basic estate plan rests on five documents—will, durable power of attorney, healthcare directive, beneficiary designations, and (optionally) a revocable living trust.
  - Titles and beneficiary forms can override what a will says, making beneficiary review at least a yearly task.
  - The 2025 federal rules cited are a $19,000 annual gift exclusion per recipient and a $13.99M federal estate exemption per person.
  - Estate planning is explicitly framed as not only for the ultra-wealthy.

actionSteps:
  - Confirm you have all five items in the Estate Planning Stack, or identify which ones are missing.
  - Check when you last reviewed your named beneficiaries on retirement accounts and life insurance.
  - Verify your account titles and estate documents are coordinated rather than conflicting.
  - Update your documents now if you've had a marriage, child, divorce, or major move since they were written.

Estate planning is easy to postpone because it sounds like something for
later, or for someone wealthier. The Big Idea on this map argues the opposite:
titles and beneficiary forms can override a will right now, which means an
outdated 401(k) beneficiary form can undo an otherwise careful plan.

The Estate Planning Stack lays out five documents in a rough order of
universality — a will and healthcare directive apply to almost every adult,
while a revocable living trust is flagged as useful specifically for
complexity or privacy, not as a default requirement.

The practical rules at the bottom are the maintenance layer: update documents
after major life changes, name backup beneficiaries, and keep titles
coordinated with the documents themselves. None of this depends on net worth.
