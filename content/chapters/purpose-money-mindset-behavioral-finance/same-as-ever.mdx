Morgan Housel's central argument is not about markets — it's about people. Technology,
products, and asset classes change constantly, but the underlying behaviors that drive
financial decisions — fear, greed, overconfidence, herd-following — show up again and
again across generations.

The five big ideas at the top of this map set the frame: human nature is slow to change,
risk is always present even when it's invisible, incentives explain behavior better than
stated intentions, uncertainty is permanent, and durable principles beat short-term
novelty. Everything below builds on that frame.

The practical playbook translates this into action: build resilience before you need it,
stress-test your assumptions, and favor flexibility over precision. The common money traps
section is really a mirror — recency bias, mistaking calm for safety, ignoring incentives,
and brittle planning are the specific ways the big ideas get ignored in practice.
