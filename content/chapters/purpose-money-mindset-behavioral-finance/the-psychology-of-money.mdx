Morgan Housel's *The Psychology of Money* argues that being good with money has
less to do with what you know and more to do with how you behave. The map
opens with five big ideas — behavior over intelligence, enough as a
superpower, time over timing, the coexistence of luck and risk, and wealth as
what stays unspent — before working down into eight concrete learnings.

The practical playbook underneath those ideas is deliberately simple:
automate saving, invest broadly and cheaply, hold a long horizon, keep
buffers, and let money buy flexibility rather than things. None of it is
technically hard. What's hard is sticking with it through market noise, social
comparison, and the pull of "keeping up."

The four traps on this map — comparison, overconfidence after wins, spending
to look rich, and assuming the recent past will repeat — are the behavioral
failure modes that undo an otherwise sound plan. Recognizing them in yourself
is most of the work.
