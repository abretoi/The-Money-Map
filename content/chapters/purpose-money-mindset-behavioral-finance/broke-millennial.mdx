keyTakeaways:
  - You don't need a perfect income or a complicated system to start improving your finances — you need to know your actual numbers.
  - An emergency cushion and a spending plan come before aggressive investing or debt payoff strategy.
  - Talking openly about money reduces the shame that otherwise keeps people stuck in avoidance.
  - Waiting for the "right" moment to start is more costly than starting imperfectly now.

actionSteps:
  - List your income, bills, and debt on paper this week, even roughly.
  - Open a separate account and save a small starter emergency cushion.
  - Give every dollar of your next paycheck a named job before you spend it.
  - Identify your single highest-interest debt and send it extra money next month.

The title is a confession, not a criticism — most people getting serious about
money for the first time are starting from behind, not from a position of
comfortable surplus. This map is built for that starting point.

Notice the order of operations in the playbook: it puts a starter emergency
cushion *before* aggressive debt payoff, and a spending plan before investing.
That sequencing matters. Attacking debt without any cushion just sets up the
next crisis to go back on a credit card.

The common traps section is worth sitting with. Shame and avoidance aren't
just unpleasant feelings — the map treats them as active obstacles that
directly cause worse financial outcomes, on par with having no plan at all.
