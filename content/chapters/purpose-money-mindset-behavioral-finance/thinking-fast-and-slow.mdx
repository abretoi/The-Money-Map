Most financial mistakes are not failures of intelligence — they are failures
of process. This map takes that claim from *Thinking, Fast and Slow* and
turns it into something usable: a short list of the specific biases that
distort money decisions, and a five-step playbook for countering them.

The Big Idea section sets up the tension: fast, intuitive thinking is
efficient but error-prone, and awareness of that fact is not enough on its
own. The Key Learnings catalogue eight specific ways judgment goes wrong —
overconfidence, loss aversion, anchoring, availability bias, narrative
appeal, neglected base rates, framing effects, and unchecked intuition.

The Practical Playbook is the map's answer to all eight at once: slow down,
codify decisions with checklists, compare against base rates, separate facts
from stories, and review outcomes afterward for bias. None of these five
steps require more intelligence — only more structure.
