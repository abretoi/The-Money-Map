Burton Malkiel's central argument is uncomfortable for anyone who enjoys
picking stocks: markets are hard to beat consistently, and most of what
determines your long-term return is decided before you ever choose a single
security.

The map compresses that argument into a practical sequence — build a
diversified portfolio, prefer low-cost index funds, automate contributions,
rebalance occasionally, and ignore the noise. None of the five steps require
predicting anything. That is the point.

The "Common Money Traps" section is really a mirror of the Key Learnings:
every trap (frequent trading, stock-picking overconfidence, high fees,
reacting to headlines) is simply what happens when discipline slips. The
takeaway isn't that indexing is clever. It's that avoiding these traps is
most of the work.
