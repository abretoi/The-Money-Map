Most money advice starts with numbers — savings rates, return targets, net worth
milestones. This map starts somewhere else: with the question of what money is
actually *for*. Jonathan Clements's framing treats every dollar as a tool in
service of a life you have already decided you want, rather than an end in
itself.

That reordering changes how the rest of the map reads. The "Key Learnings" are
not a checklist of virtues — they are guardrails against status spending,
open-ended postponement, and the trap of using net worth as a stand-in for a
well-lived life. The five-step playbook closes the loop by turning values into
a repeatable process: define, build, save, spend, review.

The traps section is worth sitting with. Each one is a version of the same
mistake — letting a proxy for the good life (a bigger number, a nicer car, a
fuller calendar of obligations) replace the actual goal.
