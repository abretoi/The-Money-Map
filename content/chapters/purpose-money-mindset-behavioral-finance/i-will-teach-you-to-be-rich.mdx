The pitch here is not "spend less" — it is "spend on purpose." The map builds
that idea into a five-part structure that moves from principle to system to
practice.

The Big Idea section sets the frame: automation beats willpower, and big
financial wins (fees, savings rate, debt, negotiation) matter far more than
small optimization tricks. The Key Learnings turn that into eight concrete
habits, and the Practical Playbook sequences them into five ordered steps —
starting with banking automation and ending with a spending plan built around
your own values.

The Common Money Traps section is worth reading twice. It doesn't just list
mistakes — it reframes each one as a systems failure rather than a willpower
failure. Waiting to feel ready, budgeting without systems, overcomplicating
investing, and cutting joy instead of waste are all, in this framing, fixable
by better defaults rather than more discipline.
