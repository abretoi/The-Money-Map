  - Emotional reactions like buying high after hype or selling low in panic tend to destroy returns.
  - Simple, automated, rule-based plans are easier to stick with than complex ones.

Carl Richards named the gap between the return investors could have earned and the return
they actually earned "the behavior gap." This map is a field guide to closing it.

The five big ideas make the diagnosis explicit: knowing isn't doing, emotions distort
decisions, simple plans are stickier than clever ones, behavior drives returns as much as
selection does, and advice only helps if it's usable. None of this is about picking better
investments — it's about sticking with reasonable ones.

The practical playbook and common money traps sections are two sides of the same coin. The
playbook — clarify goals, choose a simple allocation, automate, rebalance by rule, ignore
noise — is what discipline looks like in practice. The traps — buying high after hype,
selling low in panic, mistaking activity for progress — are what happens when that
discipline breaks down under emotional pressure.
