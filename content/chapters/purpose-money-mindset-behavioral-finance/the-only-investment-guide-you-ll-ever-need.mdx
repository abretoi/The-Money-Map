Andrew Tobias's guide resists the idea that good investing requires special
knowledge. The Big Idea section is almost defiantly plain: spend less than
you earn, avoid products you don't need, and let frugality buy you options
later.

The Practical Playbook turns that philosophy into an order of operations —
tighten savings first, then clear expensive debt, then build a safety net,
and only then invest in diversified, low-cost funds. Skipping ahead in that
sequence is exactly the kind of shortcut the map warns against.

The Common Money Traps are less about market risk and more about self-inflicted
cost: complexity, lifestyle creep, and hype. None of them require a bad market
to hurt you.
