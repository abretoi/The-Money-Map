Graham's book predates modern market data by decades, yet the map's five Big
Idea cards still describe the gap between an amateur and a disciplined
investor: knowing that price and value diverge, and having the temperament to
act on that gap rather than on a headline.

The Key Learnings section repeats a theme in different words eight times —
volatility is normal, margin of safety matters, emotions are the enemy —
because Graham considered temperament the deciding factor, not intelligence.
The map places "expect market volatility" and "stay emotionally steady" back
to back for a reason: the second is only possible once the first is accepted.

The four Common Money Traps are the practical failure modes of ignoring that
temperament: treating a rising price as proof of value, and treating a falling
one as proof of danger.
