Daniel Crosby's frame is the least numeric map in this set, and that's the
point. Where other maps optimize a rate or a ratio, this one asks a harder
question: does your money behavior match who you actually are and want to
become?

The Big Idea section separates net worth from self-worth explicitly — a
distinction easy to state and hard to hold onto when markets move. The eight
Key Learnings build the muscle for that separation: naming values, noticing
emotional patterns, and making room for reflection alongside accumulation.

The playbook is an audit-and-realign loop rather than a savings mechanism.
Name values, check current behavior against them, close the gap, and use the
resulting wealth in service of purpose rather than as an end in itself. The
traps are what happens when that loop never runs — worth gets tied to wealth,
validation gets chased externally, and "enough" never gets defined.
