The instinct to chase the highest-ranked school you can get into runs into a
harder question this map is built to answer: does rank actually predict
return? The answer, laid out across five sections, is "sometimes, and less
than most people assume."

The ROI Comparison table is the spine of the argument. Top 20 schools do post
the highest average early-career salaries, but Ranks 21–50 overlap
substantially at a meaningfully lower net price — which is why the map calls
that middle tier "often the sweet spot." Below rank 50, dispersion widens:
some cheaper schools beat pricier ones above them.

The career-path table adds the missing variable: prestige matters enormously
for investment banking and elite consulting, but much less for licensure-driven
fields like accounting, nursing, or teaching. Combined with the debt-versus-salary
test and the four practical ROI bands, the map's real argument is that the
right college is a function of your field and your debt load, not just the
ranking you can reach.
