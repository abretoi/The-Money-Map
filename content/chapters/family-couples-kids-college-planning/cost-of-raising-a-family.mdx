A family budget is easy to underestimate when it is built line by line but
never totaled. The snapshot here does both: a two-adult, two-child household
in a moderate-cost setting lands at $93,000 a year before taxes and long-term
savings — and a more resilient all-in number, once those are added, is
$110,000+.

The size comparison below the snapshot makes an important point about *when*
costs jump. It is not a smooth incline. The move from no kids to one child is
flagged as the first major jump, and two kids brings the biggest pressure on
childcare, housing, and food simultaneously.

The planning bands at the bottom turn all of this into a range rather than a
single verdict. Basic needs, comfortable, and high-cost metro are three
different realities for the same two-adult, two-child household, and the gap
between them is driven almost entirely by geography and lifestyle choices.
