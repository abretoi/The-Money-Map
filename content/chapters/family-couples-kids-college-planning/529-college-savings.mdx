keyTakeaways:
  - Starting a 529 from birth turns modest monthly contributions into large sums over 18 years — $100/month becomes about $43,072, while $750/month becomes about $323,041.
  - Private nonprofit tuition (about $45,000/year) runs well above public in-state tuition (about $11,950/year).
  - Up to $95,000 (single) or $190,000 (couple) can be front-loaded into a 529 using five years of annual gift exclusions at once.
  - Unused 529 funds aren't locked in — a lifetime rollover of up to $35,000 to a Roth IRA is allowed, subject to rules, and the beneficiary can be changed to another family member.

Tuition figures make headlines, but the more useful number on this map is what
starting early actually does to a monthly contribution. At a 7% average annual
return over 18 years, $100 a month from birth becomes about $43,072 — and
$750 a month becomes about $323,041. The multiplier isn't the contribution
size; it's time in the account.

The cost snapshot at the top gives that growth ladder context. Private
nonprofit tuition, at about $45,000 a year, dwarfs public in-state tuition at
about $11,950 — a gap wide enough that even a well-funded 529 may only cover
part of the bill, depending on which path a child takes.

The features table is where the map pushes back on the common objection —
"what if my kid doesn't go to college?" A 529 can change beneficiaries, and as
of the current rollover rules, up to $35,000 of unused funds can move into a
Roth IRA over the account's lifetime. The money is more portable than it
looks.
