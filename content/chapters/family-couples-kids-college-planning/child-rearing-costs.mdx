keyTakeaways:
  - Raising one child to age 18 is estimated at about $310,605, and this figure does not include college costs.
  - Average daycare runs about $321/week ($16,692/year), while infant care averages higher at about $376/week.
  - Housing (29%) and food (18%) together account for nearly half of child-rearing spending, ahead of childcare & education (16%).
  - Costs peak in the earliest years (ages 0–2, at $18k–$28k/year) due to infant gear and daycare, and stay elevated through the teen years.

actionSteps:
  - Budget separately for daycare/infant care against the $321/week and $376/week benchmarks if you have young children.
  - Check your own spending against the six category shares — housing 29%, food 18%, childcare & education 16%, transportation 15%, healthcare 9%, clothing & misc 13%.
  - Open or fund a Dependent Care FSA and a 529 plan as two of the specific planning tools this map names.
  - Revisit your term life insurance and emergency fund now that ongoing dependent costs exist.

The headline number on this map — about $310,605 to raise one child to age
18 — is deliberately paired with a note that college costs are not included.
That's the first lesson: even a large lifetime estimate understates the full
picture for families planning to fund higher education too.

The category breakdown shows where that money actually goes: housing (29%)
and food (18%) dominate, ahead of childcare and education (16%),
transportation (15%), clothing and misc (13%), and healthcare (9%). The
age-stage bands add a second dimension — costs are highest in the earliest
years, driven by daycare and infant gear, dip slightly in the middle
childhood years, then climb again in the teen years.

The planning tools and parent checklist sections turn those figures into
action: a Dependent Care FSA and 529 plan target the two biggest
child-specific costs directly, while term life insurance and a bigger
emergency fund protect the household against the risk that income disappears
while these costs are still running.
