keyTakeaways:
  - The 2025 tax ladder has seven brackets from 10% to 37%, with married-filing-jointly thresholds roughly double the single thresholds at most levels.
  - Long-term capital gains and qualified dividends share the same 0%, 15%, and 20% rate structure, separate from the ordinary income ladder.
  - Tax-loss harvesting can offset up to $3,000 per year of ordinary income ($1,500 if married filing separately).
  - The retirement tax strategy sequence bridges early retirement with taxable assets, uses low-income years for Roth conversions, and preserves Roth accounts for later flexibility.

Taxes are one of the few costs in personal finance that respond directly to
sequencing decisions you control. This map lays out the 2025 numbers first,
then builds toward a strategy for using them.

The top two sections are reference data: the federal quick numbers
(standard deduction, contribution limits, capital gains rates) and the full
ordinary income tax ladder across all seven brackets. The Capital Gains &
Taxable Investing section adds the rules that sit alongside that ladder —
qualified dividends, tax-loss harvesting, asset location, and municipal
bonds.

The Tax-Optimization Playbook and Retirement Tax Strategy sections are where
the map turns numbers into decisions. The retirement sequence in particular —
bridge with taxable assets, convert in low-income years, withdraw
traditional strategically, preserve Roth, and watch IRMAA and RMDs — mirrors
how account type and withdrawal order can matter as much as how much is
saved. The four Practical Tax Bands at the bottom give a rough self-assessment
scale from "just getting started" to "building legacy."
