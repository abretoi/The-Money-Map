keyTakeaways:
  - The marginal rate ladder runs from 10% up to 37% across seven brackets — your marginal rate is not the rate you pay on all your income.
  - A $15,000 standard deduction shields the first slice of income from tax entirely before the ladder even applies.
  - Traditional 401(k) and HSA contributions lower taxable income now, which is most valuable while you're in a higher current bracket.
  - Roth contributions make the most sense when your current tax rate is relatively low, since you pay tax now in exchange for tax-free withdrawals later.

actionSteps:
  - Find which of the seven brackets your last dollar of income falls into.
  - Compare your current tax bracket against what you expect it to be in retirement before choosing Roth vs. traditional.
  - Check whether you are using an HSA as a tax shelter for qualified medical expenses.
  - Review your taxable brokerage account for any loss-harvesting opportunities before year-end.

The map's big idea — marginal rate does not equal effective rate — is the
single most common misunderstanding in personal tax planning. Being "in the
24% bracket" does not mean 24% of your income goes to tax; it means only the
slice of income inside that bracket is taxed at 24%.

That distinction is what makes the planning levers section useful rather than
generic. Traditional 401(k) contributions are worth the most when they're
displacing income taxed at your *highest* marginal rate — which is exactly
why the map pairs that move with "higher current brackets." Roth works in the
opposite direction: it's most valuable when your current rate is relatively
low.

Notice that tax planning here is framed as a year-round activity, not an
April scramble. Loss harvesting, bracket-aware withdrawals, and Roth timing
all depend on decisions made throughout the year, before the picture is
locked in.
