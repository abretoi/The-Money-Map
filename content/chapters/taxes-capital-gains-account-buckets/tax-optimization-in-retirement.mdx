Retirement tax planning is often reduced to a single question: how much do I
need to withdraw? This map reframes it as a coordination problem across five
differently-taxed account types, each with its own rules and its own ideal
role.

The RMD rule is the anchor most people encounter first — required
distributions generally starting at 73 today, moving to 75 for people born in
1960 or later. But the map treats that rule as one input among several, not
the organizing principle. The Tax-Bucket Comparison table makes clear that
Roth, HSA, and taxable accounts each answer a different question about
timing, flexibility, and legacy.

The Smart Withdrawal Order Framework is the practical output of that
coordination: spend cash first, use taxable brokerage strategically, fill
brackets from traditional accounts, convert to Roth in low-income years, and
preserve Roth assets for flexibility later. The repeated caution throughout —
that the "optimal order changes" and the "best plan comes from coordinating
all tax buckets together" — is the map's real thesis.
