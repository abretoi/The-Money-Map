keyTakeaways:
  - Holding an investment for more than a year moves it from ordinary-income tax rates to the lower long-term capital gains brackets.
  - The 0% LTCG bracket runs up to $48,350 of single-filer taxable income, meaning some investors owe no federal tax on long-term gains at all.
  - Tax-loss harvesting can offset up to $3,000 of ordinary income per year, on top of offsetting realized gains.
  - Step-up in basis at death is a distinct mechanism from harvesting or holding period — it resets the cost basis for heirs.

actionSteps:
  - Before selling any position, check whether it has crossed the one-year holding period.
  - Estimate which LTCG bracket (0%, 15%, or 20%) your taxable income falls into this year.
  - >-
      Review your taxable account for asset location: are bonds sitting in the taxable account instead of tax-deferred?
  - In a down market, check whether tax-loss harvesting is available before rebalancing.

Two investors can hold the identical stock, sell it on the identical day, and
owe very different amounts of tax — the only difference being how long each
one held it. The map's stat strip lays out exactly where those lines fall:
one year for short-term versus long-term treatment, and three income
thresholds that separate the 0%, 15%, and 20% long-term capital gains brackets.

The Taxable Investing Ladder ranks five tax-relevant events by how much drag
they create, from short-term trades at the costly end down to the step-up in
basis at death, which resets cost basis entirely. In between sit qualified
dividends and tax-loss harvesting — both real levers, not just consolation
prizes.

The Taxable Account Moves table is the connective layer: it maps each ladder
concept to something more concrete — buy-and-hold, index funds, asset
location — that an investor can actually implement without a tax degree.
