  - Childcare and school costs show the sharpest single jump, going from zero to "first big jump" the moment a first child arrives.

The core numbers at the top of this map put a price on something households
usually feel but rarely quantify: the average U.S. consumer unit spends
$78,535 a year, while raising a single child to 18 adds about $310,605 on top
of that — before any college costs.

The table is the useful part. It doesn't just say costs go up with family
size; it says which costs go up, and in what order. Childcare is the category
with no gradual ramp — it goes from "no childcare costs" to "first big jump"
in a single step, the moment a first child arrives. Housing and transportation
scale more gradually, one size class at a time.

The planning moves and checklist sections exist because the table describes
pressure, not a plan. Reviewing insurance, re-running withholding, and
upgrading estate documents are the actions that convert "costs went up" into
"the plan adjusted."
