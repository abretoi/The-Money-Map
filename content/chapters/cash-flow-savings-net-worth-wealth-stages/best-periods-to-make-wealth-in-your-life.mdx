Wealth building is often described as a single long race, but this map treats
it as five distinct jobs, one per decade. Each stage has a different lever
available — time in your 20s, income growth in your 30s, peak earning power
in your 40s, catch-up room in your 50s, and preservation after 60 — and the
map's priorities checklist changes accordingly.

The Compounding Advantage table makes the cost of waiting concrete: the same
discipline applied at 25 instead of 55 illustrates roughly 10.7x growth by
age 60 versus about 1.4x, purely from the extra 30 years in the market. That
gap is time, not talent.

The map's own insight lines are worth sitting with: the best decade to start
is now, even though the best decade for maximum compounding has already
passed for most readers. And wealth is rarely a single lever — it comes from
skill growth, savings rate, and time in quality assets working together.
