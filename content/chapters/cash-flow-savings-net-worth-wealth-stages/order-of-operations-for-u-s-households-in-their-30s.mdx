Most household finance advice answers "how much should I save?" This map
answers a different question: in what order should the next dollar be spent?
That ordering matters because each step captures value the next one cannot —
an employer match not captured this year is gone for good.

The sequence is front-loaded with defense. Steps 1 through 4 — starter cash,
employer match, high-APR debt, and a full emergency fund — exist before any
tax-advantaged investing begins, because a 20% card APR is a guaranteed
return that no diversified portfolio reliably beats.

Only after that foundation is in place does the map turn to compounding: HSA,
IRA, and a maxed-out 401(k), in that order, followed by taxable brokerage for
flexibility and extra goals like mortgage prepayment last. The four planning
bands compress the nine steps into a shape worth remembering — foundation,
tax-advantaged growth, flexibility, and optimization.
