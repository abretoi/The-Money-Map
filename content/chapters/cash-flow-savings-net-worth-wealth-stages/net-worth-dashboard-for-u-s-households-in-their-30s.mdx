keyTakeaways:
  - Median net worth in the 30s (age 35–44 proxy) sits at $135,600, while the mean of $549,600 is pulled far higher by top-decile households.
  - The gap between the 25th percentile ($22,200) and the 90th percentile ($956,400) is enormous, so a single "typical" number is misleading.
  - Retirement accounts and home equity commonly dominate net worth in this age range — that concentration is normal, not a red flag by itself.
  - Net worth is a stock (a snapshot), not a flow (income), and liquidity within that stock matters as much as its size.

Net worth numbers get quoted as if there's one right answer, but the spread in
this dashboard tells a different story. Between the 25th percentile ($22,200)
and the 90th percentile ($956,400), there is more than 40x difference — for
households in the same decade of life.

The six-part breakdown matters more than the single headline number. Two
households can share an identical net worth total while one is almost entirely
home equity and retirement accounts, and the other holds meaningful cash and
brokerage assets. The dashboard's own reading guide flags this directly:
liquidity still matters, even at a high net worth.

The benchmark ladder is a way to locate yourself, not a verdict. Below-zero
net worth (debt exceeding assets) is common enough in the 30s to warrant its
own rung on the ladder, and the practical wealth zones frame each range as a
stage rather than a judgment.
