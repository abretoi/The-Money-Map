Wealth-building can feel abstract until it's broken into a conversion chain: income becomes
surplus, surplus becomes capital, capital becomes cash flow, and cash flow eventually buys
freedom. Each step depends on the one before it — you can't convert surplus into capital if
there's no surplus to begin with.

The nine-rung ladder makes that chain concrete and ordered. Notice that "Capture Free Money"
(the employer match) comes before maxing out tax-advantaged accounts generally, and that
taxable wealth engines — brokerage, real estate, business equity — only enter the picture
after the core tax-advantaged portfolio is built. The order matters as much as the
individual rungs.

The illustrative allocations show how a portfolio's composition is expected to shift with
each stage: heavy on cash and debt payoff at the Starter stage, shifting toward retirement
accounts at the Builder stage, and diversifying into taxable and real assets by the
FI/Advanced stage. The five ladder rules at the bottom are less about which asset to buy and
more about sequencing discipline — get the savings rate and tax efficiency right before
reaching for anything exotic.
