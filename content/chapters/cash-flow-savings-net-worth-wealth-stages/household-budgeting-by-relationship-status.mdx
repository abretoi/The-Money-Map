A budget isn't a single template — it's a structure that shifts with household composition.
This map lines up four common archetypes side by side: a single adult, a couple without
kids, a single parent with one child, and a married household with two kids, each with an
illustrative after-tax monthly budget and category breakdown.

The clearest pattern is what happens to housing's share as households add income and people.
It falls from 35% for a single adult to 28% for a two-income, two-kid household, even as the
dollar amount roughly triples. Fixed costs like housing and utilities benefit from being
split across more earners, which is exactly what the "What Usually Changes" section names as
fixed-cost sharing.

Childcare is the other structural shift — it doesn't appear at all for the single adult or
childless couple, but consumes 15–18% of the budget once kids enter the picture. The four
budgeting rules of thumb at the bottom — automate saving, cap housing, build sinking funds,
revise quarterly — are presented as constants that apply regardless of which archetype
describes your household.
