keyTakeaways:
  - Your main financial objective shifts from building wealth in your 30s, to balancing peak earnings in your 40s, to preserving and drawing down wealth in your 50s.
  - Target savings rates rise across the decades — 20%–30%+ in your 30s up to 25%+ with catch-up contributions in your 50s.
  - Portfolio posture should shift from growth-focused/high equity in your 30s toward a balanced, risk-managed mix by your 50s.
  - >-
      The biggest risk to avoid changes each decade: under-investing in your 30s, lifestyle inflation in your 40s, and sequence-of-returns risk in your 50s.

actionSteps:
  - Identify which decade band you're in and compare your current savings rate to its target.
  - If you're in your 40s, check whether estate documents (will, POA, beneficiaries) are in place.
  - If you're in your 50s, confirm you are using available catch-up contribution limits.
  - Review your portfolio's equity exposure against the posture recommended for your decade.

The temptation with a decade-by-decade plan is to treat it as three separate
checklists. The more useful reading is as one continuous handoff: what you
build in your 30s is what your 40s has to balance against competing
priorities, and what your 40s optimizes is what your 50s has to protect.

Look closely at how the savings-rate target and portfolio posture move
together across the scorecard. The target percentage climbs while the equity
exposure recommendation becomes more conservative — you're being asked to save
a larger share of a portfolio that's taking less risk, which is exactly what
a shrinking time horizon demands.

The biggest-risk column is arguably the most actionable row on the whole map.
Each decade has a distinct failure mode, and none of them are the same
mistake twice.
