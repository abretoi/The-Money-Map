keyTakeaways:
  - Savings rate, not investment picking, is the biggest lever on how fast you reach financial independence.
  - Moving from a 20% savings rate to a 50% savings rate cuts the estimated time to FI from about 36.7 years to about 16.6 years.
  - Below a 20% savings rate, FI usually takes multiple decades; the acceleration zone starts around 30%–40%.
  - The whole ladder assumes a 5% real return, a 4% withdrawal rule, and starting from zero savings.

Most FI conversations get lost in return assumptions and asset allocation debates.
This map strips that away and isolates one variable: **savings rate**. Everything
else — the 5% real return, the 4% withdrawal rule, the $127,388 income baseline —
is held constant across all seven rungs of the ladder.

What the ladder reveals is not a straight line. Going from 10% to 20% barely
moves the needle — 51.4 years down to 36.7. But the middle of the curve is
where structural choices pay off: crossing from 30% into the 40%–50% range
compresses the timeline by over a decade.

The zones at the bottom translate the curve into a decision framework. A
starter saver and an extreme saver are not making the same trade-off, and the
map is explicit that small optimizations don't get you between zones — only
bigger structural choices do.
