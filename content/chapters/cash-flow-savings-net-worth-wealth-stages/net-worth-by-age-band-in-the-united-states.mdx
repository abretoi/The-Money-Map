Net worth benchmarks are easy to misread. A single average, pulled upward by a
small number of very wealthy households, can make an entire age band look
richer than almost anyone in it actually is. This map uses medians instead,
and it shows why that distinction matters.

The life-cycle bands trace a familiar shape: a low starting point under 35,
a steep climb through the 40s and 50s, a peak in the mid-60s to mid-70s, and a
decline afterward as households draw down savings. The 30s Detail Panel is the
map's sharpest tool — it shows that within a single age band, the gap between
the 25th and 90th percentile is more than 40x, which is a reminder that a
median is a starting reference point, not a target to hit exactly.

The "How to Use Age Benchmarks" section closes with the map's real argument:
compare your own trajectory over time, not your salary against a net worth
figure, and not your household against an average built on outliers.
