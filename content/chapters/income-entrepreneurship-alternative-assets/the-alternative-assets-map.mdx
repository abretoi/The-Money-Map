Alternative assets get pitched as either a shortcut to outsized returns or a
reckless distraction from "real" investing. This map treats them as neither —
as a sleeve of a portfolio, sized deliberately, with a scorecard for comparing
six very different categories on the same axes.

The scorecard is the map's most useful move: putting collectibles, fine art,
watches, wine and spirits, classic cars, and crypto side by side on return
potential, liquidity, carrying cost, expertise needed, and cash-flow potential
makes trade-offs visible that marketing copy usually hides. Crypto's high
liquidity looks very different next to fine art's high carrying cost and low
liquidity.

The allocation guidance and risk checklist exist because alternatives fail in
predictable ways — bought with borrowed money, undocumented, uninsured, or
purchased before the core portfolio and emergency fund exist. The four-stage
playbook at the bottom maps a realistic path from a beginner's small, low-cost
first purchase to a high-net-worth collector's estate and legacy planning.
