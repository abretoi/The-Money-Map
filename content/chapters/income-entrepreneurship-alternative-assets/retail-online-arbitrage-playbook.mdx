keyTakeaways:
  - Replacement parts and niche accessories carry the widest ROI range on the map (30-80%), but sourcing is narrower than for high-volume categories like toys or books.
  - The Starter Roadmap is sequential — research and small test buys come before scaling, and replenishing only proven winners comes last.
  - Net profit is defined as sale price minus cost of goods, marketplace fees, shipping, and returns/prep — not just the sale price.
  - Sell-through and ROI are treated as a pair; a high ROI on an item that doesn't sell is not real profitability.

The categories table looks like a simple ROI leaderboard, but the "Where to
source" and "Best marketplace" columns are doing the real work — a 30-80% ROI
on replacement parts is only real if you can actually find that inventory and
sell it on the marketplace where buyers are looking.

The Starter Roadmap and the Unit Economics formula are the two halves of
discipline this playbook asks for. The roadmap keeps early bets small until
demand and margin are proven. The formula insists that "profit" means
whatever is left after cost of goods, marketplace fees, shipping, and
returns/prep — not the number on the price tag.

The Golden Rules close the loop: start narrow, know your fees before you buy,
and track ROI and sell-through as a pair rather than chasing high-margin
items that never move.
