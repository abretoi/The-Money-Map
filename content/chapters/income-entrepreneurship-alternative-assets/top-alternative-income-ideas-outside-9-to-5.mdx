keyTakeaways:
  - Digital products and templates are singled out as the highest-margin idea, keeping 80–95% of what you sell.
  - Freelance consulting is the fastest to start, with income possible within weeks.
  - Building a business, agency, or brand carries the biggest upside but is harder to build than the other paths.
  - The five-step launch roadmap starts with validating a single niche problem with a paying customer before building anything else.

actionSteps:
  - Pick one niche problem you could solve, rather than brainstorming a list of ideas.
  - Find one person willing to pay you for that solution before building anything further.
  - Compare your idea against the five traits of a great side income idea (low startup cost, clear customer need, repeatable demand, good margins, scalable delivery).
  - Commit to one idea for a defined period before considering a second one.

Side-income lists usually stop at "here are some ideas." This map goes one
layer deeper: it defines what makes an idea *good* before listing any ideas
at all, then scores eight of them against the same five criteria so they can
actually be compared.

The table is the core of the map — eight paths, each rated on how it works,
revenue potential, profit margin, and market opportunity. The spread is
wide: an e-commerce brand can generate more raw revenue than freelancing,
but freelancing and digital products keep a larger share of every dollar as
profit. Fastest to start, highest margin, biggest upside, and most passive
are four different questions, and the map is explicit that no single idea
wins on all four.

The Launch Roadmap and Reality Check sections are the corrective to
side-income hype: validate with one paying customer before building
anything, and expect consistency — not intensity — to be what actually
compounds.
