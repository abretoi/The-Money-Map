Retirement savings advice often collapses into "max out your 401(k)." This map
resists that shortcut. Six accounts appear side by side — Traditional 401(k),
Roth 401(k), Traditional IRA, Roth IRA, HSA, and taxable brokerage — because
each one solves a different problem: tax treatment now versus later, medical
flexibility, or no shelter at all in exchange for total access.

The comparison table turns those six accounts into hard numbers: contribution
ceilings, how each is taxed, and what accessing the money actually requires.
Notice that the HSA and taxable brokerage sit at opposite ends of the
flexibility spectrum — one triple-tax-advantaged but medically gated, the other
untaxed at contribution but fully liquid at any age.

The funding route at the bottom is the map's answer to "in what order?" — match
first, then HSA, then IRA, then finish the 401(k) before taxable investing.
It's a sequence built for households still assembling their account stack in
their 30s, not a universal rule for every income level or goal.
