FIRE math is simple in theory — multiply annual spending by 25 (the 4% rule)
or about 28.6 (the 3.5% rule) — but seeing where a typical household actually
sits against that target is more useful than the formula alone. At average
age-35-44 spending of $91,229/year, the two targets are $2,280,725 and
$2,606,543.

The milestone ladder breaks that target into five funding stages, from
$228,073 at 10% funded up to the full $2,280,725 at 100%. Against that ladder,
the current median net worth for this age group — $135,600 — sits at just
5.9% of the target, which the map is careful to frame as "well below full FI,
but on the way" rather than as a failure.

The FIRE Target Table is the piece that makes the whole picture personal:
lifestyle choice moves the target by millions of dollars, from $1,500,000 for
a lean-ish $60,000/yr life to $3,000,000 for a $120,000/yr comfort lifestyle.
Coast FIRE — about $527,708 today — offers a middle path, a balance that
could compound to the full target by 65 without any further contributions.
