  - Going from a child-free household to three children moves the illustrative FIRE-at-4.0% target from $2.00M to $3.28M.
  - Childcare years are flagged as the toughest cash-flow phase, not the whole parenting timeline.
  - Coast or Barista FIRE is positioned specifically as a strategy for the heavy child-expense years, not as the end goal.

The FIRE formula itself never changes: portfolio divided by withdrawal rate.
What changes for a family is the number being divided — annual spending — and
the child-cost data on this map shows exactly how much that number moves.

The comparison table turns an abstract cost estimate into a concrete FIRE
target. Going from child-free to three children does not scale linearly; it
compounds through childcare, housing, and food all rising together, pushing
the FIRE-at-4.0% number from $2.00M to $3.28M.

The back half of the map is about surviving the middle of that curve rather
than avoiding it. Front-loading investments before kids, automating separate
savings streams, and reassessing once daycare ends are all ways of managing
the pressure without abandoning the plan.
