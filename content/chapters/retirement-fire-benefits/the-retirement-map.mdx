keyTakeaways:
  - Retirement is a timeline of dated decisions, not a single savings target.
  - Claiming Social Security at 70 instead of 62 can raise the monthly benefit substantially, but the right age depends on longevity, other income, and spousal considerations.
  - Which account you withdraw from matters as much as how much you withdraw.
  - The low-income years between retiring and claiming Social Security are the widest window for Roth conversions.

actionSteps:
  - Write down the calendar year you reach each age milestone on the timeline.
  - Sort your accounts into the four income buckets and note the balance in each.
  - Identify whether you will have a low-income bridge period, and how many years it lasts.
  - Check your Social Security statement for your actual FRA and projected benefit at 62, FRA, and 70.

Most retirement planning collapses into a single question: *how much do I need?*
That framing hides the harder problem. Retirement is a **sequence of dated
decisions**, and several of them are irreversible.

The timeline below is the spine of the whole map. Each age opens or closes a
door — catch-up contributions at 50, the earliest Social Security claim at 62,
Medicare at 65, maximum delayed credits at 70, required distributions in the
mid-seventies. Miss the window and the option is simply gone.

The second half of the map is about *sequencing*: which bucket you draw from,
in which year, and what that does to your tax bracket and your Medicare
premiums. Two households with identical balances can end up with materially
different after-tax income depending only on withdrawal order.
