keyTakeaways:
  - Payment history (35%) and amounts owed (30%) together make up the majority of a FICO score.
  - FICO scores run from 300 to 850, split into five bands from Poor to Exceptional, each with materially different borrowing terms.
  - Keeping utilization under 30%, and ideally under 10%, is one of the highest-leverage credit health moves available.
  - Closing your oldest credit card can shorten your average account age, which works against you since older accounts support stability.

actionSteps:
  - Check where your current score falls on the five-band ladder from Poor to Exceptional.
  - Calculate your current utilization rate and compare it to the under-30%/ideal-under-10% target.
  - Before applying for a major loan, pay down balances to lower utilization first.
  - Pull your credit reports and dispute any errors you find.

Two factors — payment history and amounts owed — account for 65% of a FICO
score between them. Everything else on this map (account age, inquiries,
credit mix) is real, but secondary next to those two levers.

The five-band ladder is worth internalizing because it's not linear in
consequence. The jump from Fair to Good changes what loans you can access at
all; the jump from Very Good to Exceptional mostly changes how good the rate
is. Knowing which jump you're closest to tells you where to focus.

The practical moves section resists a common instinct: closing old, unused
cards feels like tidying up, but it shortens your average account age and can
work against the score you're trying to protect.
