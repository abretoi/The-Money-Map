keyTakeaways:
  - Credit card debt at ~21% APR average sits at the top of the danger ladder and should usually be attacked first.
  - Mortgage and other low-rate debt (<6.5%) carries the lowest urgency; extra payments there are optional, not required.
  - Avalanche minimizes total interest paid; snowball trades some interest cost for behavioral motivation.
  - A hybrid approach — clear one small debt first, then switch to avalanche — captures both the psychological win and the math.

actionSteps:
  - List every debt you carry along with its interest rate and rank it on the APR Danger Ladder.
  - Decide whether avalanche, snowball, or hybrid best fits your motivation style, then commit to it.
  - Confirm you are never missing a minimum payment on any account, regardless of payoff strategy.
  - Before refinancing anything, check that rate, fees, and risk all genuinely improve — not just one of the three.

Not all debt deserves the same urgency. The map's APR Danger Ladder ranks
five common debt types purely by rate — credit cards at 18%-29%+ down to
mortgages under 6.5% — and the ranking doubles as a payoff order: attack the
top of the ladder first.

The Avalanche vs. Snowball table makes an important distinction between the
mathematically optimal strategy and the one that is easiest to stick with.
Avalanche pays the least interest overall; snowball produces faster small
wins that keep motivation up. The hybrid approach is the map's attempt to
have both.

The four Practical Debt Rules at the bottom are less about strategy and more
about discipline: never miss a minimum, don't refinance on rate alone, and
don't add new debt while paying down old debt.
