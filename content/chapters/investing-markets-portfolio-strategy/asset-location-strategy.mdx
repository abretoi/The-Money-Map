keyTakeaways:
  - Asset location is a distinct decision from asset allocation — it is about which account holds an asset, not how much of it you own.
  - Bonds and REITs are best placed in tax-deferred accounts because their income is typically taxed at ordinary income rates.
  - High-growth stock funds and small-cap equity fit best in Roth space, where the largest gains compound tax-free.
  - Broad, low-turnover ETFs and municipal bonds are the natural fit for taxable brokerage accounts.

actionSteps:
  - List your current holdings and check whether bonds and REITs sit in a tax-deferred account.
  - Confirm your highest-growth holdings are in Roth space rather than taxable or tax-deferred accounts.
  - Review your taxable brokerage account for broad ETFs and municipal bonds rather than high-turnover funds.
  - Remember that asset allocation comes first — treat location as optimization on top of an allocation you've already decided.

Two portfolios can hold the identical mix of stocks and bonds and still
produce different after-tax returns, because the accounts holding each asset
matter as much as the assets themselves. This map is about that second
decision: not what to own, but where to own it.

The three-account map splits the work by tax character. Tax-deferred accounts
absorb the assets that generate the most ordinary-income tax drag — bonds and
REITs. Roth accounts get first claim on the assets with the highest expected
growth, since Roth gains are never taxed again. Taxable brokerage is left with
what tolerates taxation well: broad, low-turnover ETFs and municipal bonds.

The four practical rules close with the most important caveat: none of this
matters until the asset allocation itself is decided. Location is the
second-order optimization applied on top of a first-order decision about how
much to own of each asset class.
