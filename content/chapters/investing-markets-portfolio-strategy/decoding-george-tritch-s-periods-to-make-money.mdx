A 19th-century market-cycle framework showing up in a modern financial map is
an odd sight, and the map treats it that way — as a historical lens, not a
system to trade on. That framing matters more than the cycle itself.

The three-period structure (A: panic, B: good times, C: hard times) each
carries its own classical rhythm and its own modern translation. The
translations are deliberately behavioral rather than mechanical: defend in
panic, don't confuse euphoria with safety in good times, and buy when fear is
highest if fundamentals hold in hard times.

The most useful part of the map may be the side-by-side list of what the
framework gets right against what it misses. Cyclicality and extreme
sentiment are real; exact dates and immunity to macro shocks are not. The
closing "All-Season Mode" reconciles the two — use the cycle as a sentiment
overlay while still investing systematically.
