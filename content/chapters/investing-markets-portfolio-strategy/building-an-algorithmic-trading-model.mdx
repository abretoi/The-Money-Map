Building a systematic trading model is less about finding one clever signal
and more about assembling a pipeline: define a universe, build a clean data
feed, generate signals, wrap them in a risk engine, then execute and review.
Skip any one stage and the whole system is fragile, no matter how good the
underlying idea is.

The illustrative signal stack shown here — trend following and relative
strength carrying the most weight, with quality, volatility control, macro
filters, and a small mean-reversion sleeve underneath — is explicitly
illustrative. The weights are a starting point for reasoning about signal
diversification, not a formula to copy.

The risk engine and backtesting checklist are where most amateur systems
fail. A model that isn't validated out-of-sample, walk-forward tested, and
evaluated with realistic slippage and fees isn't really tested — it's
overfit to history. The non-negotiable truths at the bottom of the map exist
precisely to guard against that: robustness over curve fitting, and no
illusion that any edge lasts forever.
