keyTakeaways:
  - The map treats valuation, trend, liquidity, sentiment, and earnings as the five inputs that jointly define market regimes.
  - The best buying environments are fearful corrections, early bull recoveries, high-quality pullbacks in an uptrend, and policy or liquidity tailwinds.
  - The best selling or trimming environments are euphoric melt-ups, a broken thesis, weakening breadth, and an oversized position.
  - The signal dashboard reads six indicators — three rising toward bullish (P/E, earnings revisions, moving average, breadth) and two falling toward bullish (VIX, Fed/liquidity trend).

actionSteps:
  - Before buying or selling, check whether price, fundamentals, and sentiment actually align rather than acting on one signal alone.
  - Scale into new positions gradually instead of committing all capital at once.
  - Set a rule in advance for what "position too large" means for your own portfolio, so concentration risk is caught before it becomes a problem.
  - Separate your long-term investing decisions from any short-term trading decisions so the two timeframes don't get mixed together.

Timing markets perfectly is not the goal here — the map says as much in its
own closing line. What it offers instead is a framework for reading which
regime the market is in, so that buying and selling decisions are made with
some structure rather than on impulse.

The Core Market Truth section lists five forces — valuation, trend,
liquidity, sentiment, and earnings — that the rest of the map keeps returning
to. The Best Buying and Best Trimming/Selling sections describe the
conditions under which each force tends to align in your favor or against it,
and the Signal Dashboard turns that into six trackable indicators with a
bullish/neutral/caution read for each.

The Practical Playbook is where the map gets concrete: scale in instead of
going all-in, trim in stages instead of guessing tops, and use several signals
together rather than any single one. The closing line is the map's real
thesis — process, applied consistently, beats trying to predict the exact
bottom or top.
