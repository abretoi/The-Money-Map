keyTakeaways:
  - The allocation ladder runs from 90% stocks / 10% bonds / 0% cash (Ultra Growth) down to 40% stocks / 55% bonds / 5% cash (Conservative).
  - Cash sits around 5% in most tiers below Ultra Growth — it plays a liquidity role, not a growth role.
  - International stocks are typically 20%-40% of the equity sleeve, specifically to reduce home-country concentration.
  - Rebalancing is triggered on a schedule (once or twice a year) or by drift (about 5 percentage points off target), not by market mood.

Portfolio construction gets treated like a single decision — pick a fund, be
done. The map below breaks it into two separate questions: how much risk
should you carry given your time horizon, and what job does each asset class
actually do inside that mix.

The Allocation Ladder answers the first question with five concrete
profiles, each defined by an exact stocks/bonds/cash split rather than a
vague label. Ultra Growth and Conservative are the same idea at opposite
ends: more stocks buys more expected growth and more volatility, more bonds
buys a smoother ride and less upside.

The Diversification Roles table answers the second question. Stocks are the
growth engine, international exposure guards against home-country
concentration, bonds dampen volatility, and cash provides liquidity for
near-term needs. None of these roles is optional — a portfolio missing one
is not more aggressive, it is just less diversified.

The Practical Allocation Rules at the bottom are a check on behavior, not
math: the best allocation is worthless if it gets abandoned during a
drawdown.
