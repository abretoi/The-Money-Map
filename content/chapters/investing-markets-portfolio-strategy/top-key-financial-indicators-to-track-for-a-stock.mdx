keyTakeaways:
  - No single metric decides a stock; the five families are read together.
  - Quality and price are separate questions — a great business can still be a poor investment at the wrong price.

  - Price and volume confirm or contradict what the fundamentals suggest.

Reading a stock is not about finding one magic number. It is about triangulating
across **five families** — is the business growing, is it profitable, is it
financially sturdy, is the price sensible, and is the market confirming the
story?

The dashboard turns that into a checklist. Each metric has a "why it matters"
and a "what strong looks like," so a figure means something only in context: a
low P/E is encouraging next to durable growth and alarming next to falling
margins.

The valuation-vs-quality map is the synthesis. Quality and price are two
independent axes, and the most expensive mistake in investing lives in the
bottom-left: a stock that looks cheap precisely because the business is
deteriorating.
