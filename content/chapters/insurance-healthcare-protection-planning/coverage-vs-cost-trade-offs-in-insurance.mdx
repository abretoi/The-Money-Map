keyTakeaways:
  - Premium and out-of-pocket cost move in opposite directions — a lower premium buys a higher deductible.
  - The metal tier is really a bet on how much care you expect to use.
  - Silver is often the best value specifically when you qualify for a subsidy.
  - An HDHP+HSA rewards low-usage households who can cash-flow expenses and want the tax-advantaged account.

actionSteps:
  - Estimate your expected usage for the year — rare, moderate, or high.
  - Run the decision checklist honestly, especially cash reserve and chronic conditions.
  - Check whether you qualify for a subsidy before dismissing Silver.
  - If usage is low and you have reserves, price out an HDHP+HSA against a low metal tier.

Every health plan makes the same trade: pay more each month, or pay more when
you actually use care. The metal tiers simply price that trade at different
points — Bronze covers about 60% for the lowest premium, Platinum about 90% for
the highest.

That means choosing a plan is really a forecast of your own usage. Someone who
rarely sees a doctor and has cash reserves is overpaying for Platinum; someone
managing a chronic condition may find Bronze's deductible brutal.

The HDHP+HSA sits slightly outside the metal ladder: a high deductible in
exchange for a triple-tax-advantaged account. It rewards low-usage households
who can absorb costs up front — and it is the one option here that also builds a
long-term savings asset.
