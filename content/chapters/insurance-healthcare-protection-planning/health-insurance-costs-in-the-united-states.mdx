Health insurance costs are usually understood as a single number — the
premium — when in fact four separate layers determine what a household
actually spends in a year. This map lays out those layers in order and
attaches the current planning figures to each.

Premium, deductible, copays/coinsurance, and out-of-pocket max stack
sequentially: the premium keeps coverage active, the deductible is paid first
out-of-pocket, copays and coinsurance are shared with the plan after that,
and the out-of-pocket max caps total yearly exposure — after which the plan
covers 100%.

The coverage-path comparison matters because cost traits differ sharply
across employer plans, the ACA Marketplace, Medicaid, and HDHP+HSA setups.
And usage pattern changes who "loses" on any given plan: a low-usage healthy
adult tends to overpay in premiums relative to care used, while a high-usage
chronic-care household is exposed on the out-of-pocket side instead.
