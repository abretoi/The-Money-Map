keyTakeaways:
  - Healthcare planning priorities shift by life stage — foundation-building in your 20s and 30s, family coverage needs in your 30s and 40s, HSA maximization and long-term care planning in your 50s and early 60s, and Medicare coordination at 65+.
  - The 2025 HSA limits are $4,300 for self-only coverage and $8,550 for family coverage, with an extra $1,000 catch-up at age 55+.
  - Medicare eligibility generally starts at 65, with a 2025 Part B premium of $185 per month.
  - The ACA out-of-pocket maximum for 2025 is $9,200 individual and $18,400 family.

actionSteps:
  - Confirm your HSA contributions are on track to hit the 2025 limit for your coverage type ($4,300 self or $8,550 family).
  - If you are 55 or older, check whether you're using the extra $1,000 HSA catch-up contribution.
  - If you're in your 30s or 40s, review whether your emergency fund covers 6–12 months of expenses including healthcare deductibles.
  - If you're approaching 65, mark your Initial Enrollment Period for Medicare on your calendar to avoid late-enrollment penalties.

Healthcare costs don't arrive as a single lump sum to plan for once — they
shift in character at each life stage, and this map organizes that shift into
four bands running from a 20s foundation to a 65+ Medicare transition.

The early bands are about building capacity before it's needed: preventive
care, an HSA while eligible, and disability insurance to protect the income
that funds everything else. The middle bands add family-specific
complexity — pediatric and maternity needs, broader provider networks, and a
larger emergency fund once deductibles apply to a whole household.

The 65+ band is the most procedural, because Medicare enrollment carries real
penalties for missing deadlines. Part B is required to avoid late-enrollment
penalties, and the 2025 premium of $185 per month is the concrete number tying
that requirement to a specific cost.
