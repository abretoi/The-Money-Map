  - The stack targets health, disability, term life, property/liability, and umbrella coverage in that order of priority.
  - Term life insurance is commonly sized around 10x annual income; at the stated baseline that's about $1.27M.
  - Disability insurance is sized around 60% of pre-tax income to protect the paycheck itself, not just dependents.
  - The umbrella policy sits on top of home and auto limits, commonly starting at $1M, and matters more as net worth rises.

  - Calculate 10x your annual income to check your term life insurance target.

  - Confirm your auto, home, or renters policy has adequate limits and replacement cost coverage.
  - If your net worth is rising, evaluate whether a $1M+ umbrella policy is appropriate.

Insurance decisions tend to get made piecemeal — a policy added here, a
deductible adjusted there — without a sense of the whole stack. This map lays
out five layers in order: health, disability, term life, property/liability,
and umbrella, each sized against a stated income baseline.

The sizing rules of thumb are worth noticing because they're specific:
roughly 10x income for term life, roughly 60% of pre-tax income for
disability. Against the $127,388/year baseline shown, those translate to
about $1.27M in life insurance and about $6,369/month in disability coverage
— concrete enough to check your own numbers against.

The "Big Rule" is the filter that keeps the stack from becoming
over-engineered: insure against catastrophic loss, not routine costs you
could absorb yourself. That's also why the umbrella layer — protection above
your home and auto limits — is framed as something that matters more as net
worth rises, not a universal starting point.
