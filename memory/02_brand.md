# 02 · Brand

Treasure map, navigation, discovery, milestones. **Never cheesy.** No skulls, no
parrots, no pieces-of-eight, no "Ahoy". The reference points are Apple, Linear,
Raycast, Vercel, Arc, and Stripe — with cartography as texture, not costume.

## The gold rule

`--gold` marks **treasure** — and only treasure. Two things qualify:

1. **Earned states** — progress complete, unlocks, milestones, badges, the
   closing "core lesson" a reader reaches at the end of a map.
2. **Cartographic vocabulary** — the map's own language: island/place names,
   coordinate-like labels (age milestones on a timeline), the compass motif.
   These are the treasure map, so they may wear gold.

Everything else must not: buttons, links, borders, backgrounds, nav chrome,
body text, generic accents. Green is the accent; gold is treasure.

This is the single constraint that keeps the theme sophisticated. A brand-new
reader still sees some gold (the map is gold), so the treasure aesthetic reads
immediately — but gold never degrades into a decorative accent color, which is
the failure mode that turns the pirate theme into costume. Do not relax the
"never generic chrome" half of this rule.

## Palette

Black → white surface ladder. Green is the single semantic accent, expressed as
a gradient range (`--green-1` deep → `--green-3` bright). Full tokens in
`styles/tokens.css`.

## Voice

Plain, specific, unhurried. Explain sequencing and trade-offs rather than
prescribing. Never hype, never urgency, never "secrets" or "hacks". Assume the
reader is intelligent and busy.
